Story 01
Government & Utilities
Winning a Power Authority That Hadn’t Changed Vendors in Six Years

The Pursuit
I had visited their procurement office three times over two months and been turned away each time. Their existing ISP had been in place for years, and the IT manager saw no reason to change. Rather than pushing, I asked for just 15 minutes to show something new — not a sales pitch, but a live monitoring demo on their type of infrastructure.
Building Interest
I brought a tablet loaded with our EMS dashboard and showed real-time environment data from a similar facility we’d already connected. When the Chief Engineer saw temperature alerts and power fluctuation logs he didn’t know were possible remotely, the tone shifted entirely. We had their attention.
The Difficulties
Our fiber route to their secondary substation hit a delay due to road-digging permissions. Simultaneously, their internal security committee raised concerns about cloud data storage. I coordinated between our NOC team and their IT security leads weekly, presenting a private-cloud alternative that cleared their compliance requirements. We went live three weeks behind schedule — but within the agreed SLA window.
“The client expanded to 2 additional substations within 12 months. Reported 99.8% uptime in Year 1 — up from ~91% with their previous provider. Contract value grew 3x from the original deal.”

The Pursuit
Their procurement head dismissed my first two emails. I noticed on LinkedIn that their local IT Administrator had recently posted about connectivity frustrations during a video call outage. I reached out to him directly with a specific solution — not a brochure — and he agreed to a casual conversation. He became my internal champion.
Making Them Say Yes
I proposed a 30-day parallel trial — we’d run alongside their existing provider at no cost so they could compare uptime and latency themselves. The numbers spoke clearly. After three weeks, they asked to fast-track the switchover. I packaged dedicated internet with Microsoft 365 licenses and IP telephony into a single monthly invoice, which simplified their accounting significantly.
The Difficulties
During Microsoft tenant migration, their existing email archives conflicted with the new domain setup. Our technical team and I stayed connected over three consecutive evenings to resolve it without business-hour disruption. My own company’s provisioning team initially missed the go-live date by two days — I personally escalated to the NOC head and managed the client relationship during the gap.
“Connectivity costs reduced by 23% vs their previous vendor. Microsoft 365 deployed for 45+ users with zero data loss. The IT Administrator told me: ‘You’re the first vendor who stayed past 9pm to fix something that wasn’t your fault.’”

The Pursuit
A food manufacturing group was mid-contract with a competitor but visibly frustrated — their factory floor operations were suffering from intermittent connectivity. A mutual contact flagged me. My first meeting, I said nothing about our products. I spent 40 minutes asking what had gone wrong and what they actually needed. They had never been asked that question by a vendor before.
Winning the Requirement
Based on that conversation I mapped their exact need: redundant dedicated internet for two factory floors, VM hosting for their ERP system, and a basic cybersecurity layer. I wrote a custom SLA with penalty clauses — something I pushed hard internally to get approved — because I knew that was the only language that would rebuild their trust. They signed within two weeks.
The Difficulties
Midway through deployment, the client’s Operations Director changed scope and asked to add a third location without adjusting the timeline. Internally, our delivery team pushed back. I negotiated a phased rollout — the two original floors on time, the third within 21 extra days — and wrote that into an addendum the same day to prevent further changes. It held.
“Renewed at 12 months without a single negotiation. Referred 2 sister companies the following quarter. Combined contract value from this one relationship: 4x the original deal.”

Education & NGO
Closing a Budget-Constrained Institution by Making the Numbers Work Differently
01

02

03

The Pursuit
I met their Managing Director at a business networking event and we spoke briefly about digital infrastructure for education. He gave me his card but warned me upfront: “We have a very tight budget.” Rather than treating that as a wall, I treated it as a design constraint. I went back to my desk and built a package from the ground up around their specific budget ceiling.
Getting to Yes
Over three rounds with their finance team I itemised what they were currently spending across separate vendors for internet, hosting, domain, SSL, and email — and showed that our bundled price was 18% lower total. The conversation shifted from “can we afford this” to “why are we paying more for less right now.”
The Difficulties
Their domain migration hit a critical issue — their old registrar had locked the domain due to an unpaid renewal, and the timing clashed with their student enrollment season. Any downtime would have meant hundreds of application emails bouncing. I coordinated an emergency unlock process through our technical team and their registrar over 36 hours, ensuring zero downtime during their peak period.
“Total service cost 18% lower than their previous multi-vendor setup. Zero email downtime during the critical enrollment period. The MD called personally — ‘smoothest vendor transition we’ve ever done.’”
